# Reviewing vision and contracts

Help an owner find guidance that will mislead, stall, or fail its users. Review the [vision](../docs/VISION.md) and [contracts](../README.md#the-contracts) as the governing method. This guide supports a human reviewer or an agent assigned that role.

A review offers evidence and judgment. It does not silently become an approval gate, grant the reviewer authority over the project, or establish that a system works.

## Read as a new participant

Begin with the target project's vision, contracts, and their explicit references. Try to explain the intended outcome, who it serves, the essential promises, and what remains open. Do not fill gaps with conversation history the next participant will not have.

Distinguish a missing promise, a deliberate design commitment, and an implementation choice left to an owner. Judge by meaning, not matching headings, file counts, vocabulary, or a document template.

Review the next proposed outcome, not just the documents in isolation. Identify unresolved choices that would materially change what gets built or how contributions fit. Help formulate the missing agreement, and say which implementation depends on it. Do not treat every open detail as a reason to stop or require a complete specification before useful exploration.

## Walk through realistic situations

| Situation | What the guidance should let you determine |
| --- | --- |
| Starting with an idea | How to clarify intent without treating exploration as permission to implement everything. |
| Joining unfinished work | Where to find the current agreement, existing work, authority, and evidence. |
| Being asked to build before direction is clear | Which omissions or conflicting interpretations need resolution, who can resolve them, and what work can still proceed. |
| Choosing the next work | What gap matters, who owns the outcome, what completion means, and when to stop. |
| Making an ordinary reversible choice | Why the owner can proceed and verify without asking again. |
| Making a consequential delegated choice | What conditions and evidence permit action without a new approval. |
| Making a technology or architecture bet | What is chosen, why, where it applies, what remains uncertain, and what evidence would prompt reconsideration. |
| Defining a shared client experience | Which interactions, settings, defaults, and visual details must match across platforms, and which may adapt. |
| Reaching a shared boundary | What others rely on, when their agreement is needed, and who checks the combined result. |
| Losing a tool, worker, or confirmation | How to investigate, retain progress, and avoid duplicate effects. |
| Hearing “done” | Which user journeys and combined behavior were checked, against which delivered version. |
| Changing direction | How the agreement changes and affected work receives it. |

Repeat the decision cases with a person available and unavailable. Availability should change communication timing, not delegated authority. Follow one assignment through interruption and handoff.

For a portable methodology, also check whether equivalent capabilities can fulfill it in different agent environments. A project may intentionally choose a technology or protocol for a stated reason; check its scope and supporting evidence rather than rejecting specificity. The general methodology stays portable across those choices.

## Challenge what will cause trouble

Look for a promise nobody can check, an unnamed owner, conflicting copies of an agreement, an unexplained design mandate or an example mistaken for a requirement, or a guide containing an essential obligation absent from the contracts.

Flag wording that asks people to approve every dependency touch, ordinary edit, missing test result, or already-delegated decision. Conversely, a reversible choice is not automatically authorized if it changes another owner's promise or exceeds the assignment.

Check review effort too. Can a reader find what matters, inspect evidence when needed, and challenge a decision without reading an activity log? Prefer a useful example to another abstract paragraph. Recommend a split when different responsibilities need separate attention; avoid mechanical page limits.

## Return a useful finding

For each material issue, provide:

- **Location and claim:** the precise passage and what it appears to require.
- **Failure example:** a realistic choice or action it would mishandle.
- **Consequence:** confusion, unmet behavior, lost work, unnecessary waiting, or another concrete cost.
- **Smallest repair:** wording or structure that resolves it while preserving useful freedom.

Separate contradictions, missing information, and preferences. State what you inspected and what remains untested. If the documents support a sound course of action, show it rather than inventing findings.

The appropriate owner decides disputed commitments. Apply straightforward editorial fixes only within the review assignment. After a repair, repeat the failed scenario and check neighboring promises for conflict. A tabletop review supports confidence in the guidance; live behavior still needs evidence.
