# Using Converge as a person

Use your attention where judgment changes the outcome. The [vision](../docs/VISION.md) and [contracts](../README.md#the-contracts) describe the shared method; this guide shows one way to put it to work.

## Start with the result

Describe who needs something and what should become better. Explain the purpose, important constraints, and what would make the result worthwhile. Invite your collaborators to question the framing.

For example: “People asking for help should always know which team is responsible.” That gives a direction without deciding the database or screen layout.

Make uncertainty visible. A sketch, example, or working mock can settle a misunderstanding faster than more prose. Identify what it demonstrates and what remains exploratory. A useful experiment need not become a commitment.

## Agree where contributions meet

Write a short vision and contracts for the promises people will rely on. In the example, “Support remains responsible until Billing accepts the request” is a useful promise. A technology choice, a common interface, or exact settings across clients can also belong when the team needs to rely on them. Explain the purpose and what is fixed versus flexible; [these examples](../docs/writing-guide.md#detail-where-it-matters) show the distinction. Leave ordinary internal choices with their owners.

Name the current agreement, who can change it, and who owns the combined result. Give each promise one home. For an uncertain design bet, agree how you will learn whether it helps and what would prompt reconsideration. Owners may carry several parts or repositories; peers collaborate on the behavior that crosses their boundaries.

Review for meaning: could someone explain the promise, challenge it, and recognize a violation? Use [the reviewer guide](reviewer-guide.md) when an independent reading would help.

## Delegate an outcome and room to act

An assignment can be brief:

> Make transfers keep a visible owner until the receiving team accepts. Include acceptance and refusal. Choose and test implementation details within the agreed design. Do not change who may see customer information. Stay within the agreed budget and return a verified result with remaining limitations.

Specify the authority and limits that matter for this work. Reversible choices within that scope proceed without another approval. Consequential choices already delegated receive proportionate care. A choice outside the assignment goes to the person or owner with the needed authority; unrelated work continues.

Your availability does not change those boundaries. You should not need to stay online to keep ordinary work moving.

## Collaborate at the useful level

Ask about the outcome, relevant tradeoffs, and evidence. Bring specialist expertise in where it changes a decision, without requiring everyone to follow every implementation detail.

When a decision needs your attention, expect the unresolved choice, evidence, options, recommendation, and consequence of delay. Challenge the question itself if it misses the real need.

When returning to work, look for what changed since your last review, what is now verified, and what genuinely needs your judgment. A long activity log is not a substitute.

## Judge and redirect

Agents exercise user journeys, check integration, and repair failures. You judge whether the result serves the need and whether the evidence deserves confidence. You may explore the product yourself, but routine testing is part of the delegated work.

Give feedback naturally: “That transfer still looks complete before anyone accepts it.” The workflow should investigate the relevant version, connect existing work, and return a result.

If the destination changes, update the agreement and reassess the gap. Starting fresh, finishing an incomplete project, and changing direction use the same questions: where are we, where do we want to be, and what is the next useful move?

As understanding improves, remove obsolete explanation and split guidance that carries different responsibilities. Keep the thinking people need to judge; remove what only makes the reading longer.
