# Feedback and learning

For people giving feedback, agents acting on it, and future participants. The promise: observations become traceable decisions and improvements, with their reasoning intact.

**Example:** “It still says transferred too soon” prompts a check of the version the person saw. The workflow finds the existing issue, fixes the behavior, strengthens its check, and reports the outcome. The person needn’t create another ticket.

## Commitments

1. **Accept feedback naturally.** A conversation, note, image, or demonstration can carry useful evidence. Preserve its meaning and source; do not make the person diagnose or classify it before investigating.

2. **Understand before assigning.** Check the relevant version and current behavior. Connect related reports, separate different causes, and reuse existing work where appropriate. A broken promise calls for a fix; a proposed change of direction calls for the agreement process in [Shared direction](shared-direction.md).

3. **Close the feedback loop.** Record what the observation led to: a correction, a decision, further investigation, or a reason for no change. “Still broken” reopens verification rather than producing another reassuring answer. Return the result to the contributor.

4. **Preserve reasoning and test its effect.** Keep what was noticed, why it mattered, what changed, and what happened afterward. Update the relevant agreement, guidance, tool, or check. Earlier decisions inform new work; current agreements govern it. When evidence challenges a design bet or reaches an agreed reconsideration point, revisit its rationale through the agreement process rather than silently replacing it.

5. **Learn about the workflow too.** Look for repeated corrections, missed dependencies, costly interruptions, and checks that gave false confidence. Change the process where it helps. Reassess whether further work is worth its attention, cost, and support burden; stop or redirect when warranted.

**Check:** Trace feedback into a decision, changed work, and an observed result. On similar later work, can the team use the lesson without asking the expert to repeat it? Preserve consequential dissent even when most reviewers agree.

**Left open:** Memory systems, record formats, and retrospective schedules. Tools and guides support these contracts; they do not add hidden obligations.
