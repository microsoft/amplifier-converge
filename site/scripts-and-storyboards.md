These independent videos help a team adapt when AI expands what each person can take on faster than the team's way of working together. Start with the human challenge: aligning larger responsibilities, choosing worthwhile investments, drawing on one another's expertise, and producing a useful whole. Individual agent practice supports that shared work.

[Watch the standalone videos and browse the scenes](https://microsoft.github.io/amplifier-converge/).

The story follows peers building a partner platform. Each owns a substantial contribution, with agent teammates helping prepare decisions and carry agreements into verified work. Scenes are illustrative. Film timings follow the recorded narration at approximately 150 words per minute.

# Video direction

Each video stands alone. Choose the human teamwork challenge, practical collaboration between peers, or carrying a shared commitment through your own agent environment.

Warm crafted visual character; ivory, forest green, orange and plum. Human roles stay recognizable while concrete decision cards and request screens carry the explanation. Selected options, reasons and consequences appear in sequence. Use team illustrations as a light human anchor, not the main content of every scene.

Warm, engaged, conversational masculine delivery at about 150 words per minute. Natural momentum and varied emphasis, with short useful pauses; neither slow and reflective nor rushed. Human peers talking through worthwhile shared work. Let the visuals carry the lists while the voice keeps moving.

# Film 01 Everyone can build more How do we build together

**Purpose:** Recognize the human teamwork challenge and see a practical way to meet it.

**Duration:** 2:29 · 370 spoken words.

## 01-01 Our capacity grew before our collaboration did

**Timing:** 0:00–0:18

**Narration**

You and two colleagues each have something impressive to show. But one built for self-service, another planned a staffed launch, and a third changed the connection the others depend on. AI helped everyone move faster. Now the team has a week of untangling to do.

**Storyboard**

Open immediately on three human-owned contributions and incompatible expectations. Experience: Self-service. Operations: Staffed launch. Access: Connection changed. Each appears ready on its own; joining them reveals a partner unable to start and repair work returning to the peers. The choices and their consequences fill the screen, with human roles consistently identified.

**On-screen text: Each part looks ready / The shared launch is not**

## 01-02 Good work can still point in different directions

**Timing:** 0:18–0:37

**Narration**

Nobody meant to create that work. Each person can now lead a much larger contribution, with agents helping build it. But the decisions reach across other people's plans. A faster team needs a better way to agree what matters, and what each person can depend on.

**Storyboard**

Keep the same three peers and show each directing a substantial contribution with agent support. A change made inside one area creates rework in a neighboring area. Replace the mismatch with a short shared-outcome card and explicit promises between contributions. Avoid repeating the opening labels as a second explanation of the same failure.

**On-screen text: Bigger contributions / Decisions affect our peers**

## 01-03 The responsibility has grown too

**Timing:** 0:37–0:55

**Narration**

We're learning to work more like leaders of connected teams. What deserves investment? Who owns the outcome? What can we rely on from each other? Greater production capacity hasn't given us more attention, or instant experience making those calls. We need each other's judgment more than ever.

**Storyboard**

Show a concrete investment choice: broad self-service launch versus a supported pilot. Compare reach and time to learn without invented metrics. Highlight the limited expert attention both choices need. The peers decide together; no central boss or automatic approval gate.

**On-screen text: Reach more now / Learn sooner / Attention is limited**

## 01-04 Put judgment where it changes the shared outcome

**Timing:** 0:55–1:16

**Narration**

The Converge methodology is a shared way of working, using the agents and tools you already have. Agree on a worthwhile outcome. Give people complete contributions to own, with room to act. Make the promises between those contributions clear. Bring expertise into important decisions, prepared well enough that a colleague can help.

**Storyboard**

Identify Converge methodology as a shared way of working. The peers choose Supported pilot because they want feedback sooner. The consequence is visible: wider rollout waits; experience, access and operations each carry a complete contribution with room to act. Use people and choice cards in place of a decorative village illustration.

**On-screen text: Converge methodology / Supported pilot / Learn first**

## 01-05 Make the agreement carry beyond the conversation

**Timing:** 1:16–1:34

**Narration**

A vision describes the outcome and experience we want. Contracts capture the promises we depend on. A working mock can make them easier to judge. The same agreements align colleagues and guide their agents, so what we decide together can shape the work after the conversation ends.

**Storyboard**

Show one small accepted vision beside a real-looking request-status mock. Vision: First partners can get help. Contract: Current team owns the request until the next team accepts. The same agreement is referenced by all three owners and their agents. Keep the screen focused on the useful artifact.

**On-screen text: First partners can get help / Ownership stays until acceptance**

## 01-06 Let capable teammates carry the follow-through

**Timing:** 1:34–1:52

**Narration**

Agents help expose missing direction, investigate choices, and carry authorized work through implementation, coordination, checking, and repair. Their capabilities need to fit the responsibility. The team still needs evidence that its contributions work together. Several impressive demos don't establish a usable service.

**Storyboard**

Animate the agreed transfer in a request-status mock. Access remains responsible while a transfer to Operations is pending. A faulty screen briefly names Operations too early; the agent detects the mismatch, repairs it and rechecks. Evidence shows the partner sees the actual responsible team.

**On-screen text: Pending: Access / Accepted: Operations / Check the visible owner**

## 01-07 Judge the result and the attention it took

**Timing:** 1:52–2:11

**Narration**

We should see the benefit in practice: useful outcomes, fewer repeated explanations, less rescue work. If a review queue keeps growing, reconsider the commitments. If the destination changes, work from where we are toward what matters now. The way we collaborate should improve as we learn.

**Storyboard**

The peers review a usable pilot and the friction of getting there. Remove a repeated explanation that a clearer promise has resolved. Set aside a lower-value initiative when specialist attention is overcommitted. Recalculate the next move from current evidence.

**On-screen text: Useful result / Less repetition / Less rescue**

## 01-08 Practice the transition together

**Timing:** 2:11–2:29

**Narration**

Try it on a project you already share. Open the Converge methodology with your agent. Ask it to prepare one short agreement: the next useful outcome, who owns each part, and what they need from each other. Resolve the important differences together, then get moving.

**Storyboard**

Show a completed short agreement: Outcome: Partners can get help. Owners: Experience / Access / Operations. Promise: Always show the responsible team. Whole-journey check: Experience owner. End with Converge methodology and the repository link. No references to other films.

**On-screen text: Converge methodology / Apply it to your current project**

# Film 02 How do we work together at this scale

**Purpose:** Know what to agree with peers, how to contribute expertise, and how to steer a shared investment.

**Duration:** 4:04 · 603 spoken words.

## 02-01 Choose the shared bet

**Timing:** 0:00–0:25

**Narration**

Three colleagues are launching a service for business partners. Their agents are making fast progress. But each colleague has a different idea of ready: self-service, a staffed pilot, or a reliable system behind the scenes. The Converge methodology helps them turn those assumptions into a shared bet: get the first partners working, learn before expanding, and stay within agreed limits.

**Storyboard**

Within the opening seconds show the three peers' different definitions of Ready: Experience expects self-service; Operations expects a staffed pilot; Access expects a reliable connection. Keep their identities throughout. Name Converge methodology, then bring their cards under one outcome: first partners working, learn before expanding.

**On-screen text: Three meanings of ready / One shared outcome**

## 02-02 Give people whole contributions to own

**Timing:** 0:25–0:50

**Narration**

One colleague owns the partner experience. Another owns access and approvals. A third owns running the service. Each can carry a substantial contribution with agent support. They name an owner for the combined result, agree the promises between them, and make clear which decisions stay local and which need the affected owners. Nobody has to supervise every detail of everybody else's work.

**Storyboard**

Show three human owner cards, each with a complete outcome and local authority. Experience owns the partner journey and coordinates the combined result; Access owns entry and permissions; Operations owns support. Their agent teams carry the work. Make the shared promises visible between cards without drawing an org chart or suggesting every action requires approval.

**On-screen text: Experience / Access / Operations / Local authority**

## 02-03 Find the consequential difference early

**Timing:** 0:50–1:16

**Narration**

Their agents put the different launch assumptions side by side. Self-service could reach more partners, but needs more work before anyone can start. A staffed pilot reaches fewer people and gets feedback sooner. Agents prepare a working mock and a recommendation. The owners choose the pilot because learning comes first. Unaffected work continues while they settle that shared direction.

**Storyboard**

Animate a decision the peers can judge: Self-service / More reach / More preparation versus Staffed pilot / Fewer partners / Feedback sooner. Select the pilot with the reason Learn first. Show the consequence: broad rollout stays later while independent work continues. Mark the selected direction as agreed.

**On-screen text: Self-service: More reach / Staffed pilot: Feedback sooner / Chosen: Learn first**

## 02-04 Make the connections dependable

**Timing:** 1:16–1:42

**Narration**

A shared contract records a promise the workstreams can rely on: a request keeps a responsible team until the next team accepts it. They agree who records that change and what partners see. A chosen interface or exact experience behavior can belong here when others depend on it. Ordinary internal choices stay with the owner. Each agent workflow gets the same current agreement.

**Storyboard**

Introduce one contract and its state changes. A request shows Responsible: Access. A transfer request to Operations leaves Access responsible. Operations accepts; only then does the visible owner become Operations. This establishes the promise, not a failed test. One accepted agreement supplies both contributors.

**On-screen text: Current team owns it until the next team accepts**

## 02-05 Make a colleague easy to help

**Timing:** 1:42–2:09

**Narration**

The experience owner asks a designer to try the handoff mock. The agent supplies the goal, the promise, and the open question. She spots the problem: the screen says transferred before the next team accepts. A partner could think help is on its way when it isn't. They change it to transfer requested, keep the current team visible, and give agents that example to implement.

**Storyboard**

Label this a mock review. Show the designer comparing an actual request card with the promise. Before: Transferred / Responsible: Operations, while acceptance is still pending. Consequence: the partner believes Operations is helping. After: Transfer requested / Responsible: Access. Mark it as the agreed example for agents to implement; this is the discovery and correction of misleading wording, not another undefined handoff failure.

**On-screen text: Mock review / Transferred becomes Transfer requested / Current owner stays visible**

## 02-06 Communicate what changes the decision

**Timing:** 2:09–2:31

**Narration**

A useful update helps peers make the next investment decision. The pilot is ready for a small group. A wider launch needs more support coverage. We recommend learning from this group before expanding. The evidence and alternatives are one step away. Colleagues can judge the trade-off without reconstructing every task, and owners keep making choices within their existing authority.

**Storyboard**

Replace the activity stream with a concrete peer update: Small pilot ready; Wider launch needs more support; Recommendation: Learn with this group first. Show the choice: Start the small pilot now / Invest in wider support. The recommendation is highlighted, evidence reachable, ordinary authorized work continuing. Do not introduce another stranded-partner example.

**On-screen text: Small pilot ready / Wider launch needs support / Learn first**

## 02-07 Keep work moving within the agreements

**Timing:** 2:31–2:55

**Narration**

People and agents make choices within the authority already given. A consequential delegated choice gets proportionate investigation and checks. Changing a promise another owner relies on needs that owner's agreement. Agents prepare those decisions and keep independent work moving. If everyone needs the same expert at once, the team reprioritizes commitments and protects time for the work that needs real thought.

**Storyboard**

Show three recognizable choices and consequences: layout improved and checked locally; a delegated migration checked against its agreed limits; a proposed access-policy change prepared for its owner while independent work continues. A specialist's overfull queue causes a lower-priority commitment to be deferred, making attention limits visible.

**On-screen text: Choose and check / Coordinate changed promises / Keep independent work moving**

## 02-08 Connect early and revise together

**Timing:** 2:55–3:17

**Narration**

Now agents test the corrected experience across the real contributions. Request a transfer. Decline it: the original team still owns it. Accept it: responsibility changes, and the partner sees the new team. This checks that the agreed promise survived implementation. Agents repair any mismatch and repeat the journey. The team receives evidence of the whole working together.

**Storyboard**

Label this a connected journey check. Reuse the corrected mock's exact state labels in the running experience. Transfer requested: Access still owns. Declined: Access still owns. Accepted: Operations owns. Show each observed state checked against the one existing promise and return that evidence. No new policy disagreement or repeat of the designer's discovery.

**On-screen text: Connected journey check / Requested: Access / Declined: Access / Accepted: Operations**

## 02-09 Review the investment as well as the product

**Timing:** 3:17–3:42

**Narration**

The owners judge whether the pilot serves its purpose and whether the evidence deserves confidence. They decide what to expand, adjust, or stop within their authority. They also examine the total attention it consumed, including setup, review, corrections, and later repair. Which corrections repeated? Did advice reach the product? Was someone left to rescue the result? Use those answers to improve the next cycle of teamwork.

**Storyboard**

At a peer review, show the working result, relevant evidence, a remaining limitation, and the next investment choice. Keep the attention review concrete: a repeated misunderstanding and an unnecessary handoff. Record a change that can prevent both.

**On-screen text: Result / Investment / Attention / Next adjustment**

## 02-10 Begin with your actual collaborators

**Timing:** 3:42–4:04

**Narration**

Try the Converge methodology on work you already share. Give your agent the guidance and your project. Ask for a short agreement: one useful outcome, the owners, the promises between them, and who checks the whole. Settle consequential differences with your peers. Let owners make the choices already within their authority.

**Storyboard**

Show the completed starting agreement: Outcome: Partners can get help. Owners: Experience / Access / Operations. Promise: Always show the responsible team. Whole-journey check: Experience owner. End with Converge methodology, the repository link, and Apply it to your current project. No next-film invitation.

**On-screen text: Converge methodology / Apply it to your current project**

# Film 03 How do I carry my part through

**Purpose:** Carry a shared commitment through your own agent workflow and return a result peers can rely on.

**Duration:** 3:09 · 463 spoken words.

## 03-01 Begin from the shared agreement

**Timing:** 0:00–0:22

**Narration**

Your demo works. Your colleague still can't use it. You own the experience for a partner pilot; peers own access and operations. Give your agent the Converge methodology, shared agreements, and project access. Have it inspect reality and its tools, expose consequential gaps, and choose next steps within those agreements.

**Storyboard**

Open with a human teamwork consequence: the Experience demo is ready, but Operations cannot use it because the pending handoff behavior is missing. Establish a standalone partner-pilot example with You: Experience and Peers: Access and Operations. Name Converge methodology. Show the agent inspecting the existing agreement—current ownership stays until acceptance—then choosing to implement the missing state within that agreement.

**On-screen text: Demo ready / Peer cannot use it / Converge methodology**

## 03-02 Delegate a complete result

**Timing:** 0:22–0:46

**Narration**

Make the pilot journey ready, including acceptance, refusal, and handoff. Choose implementation details within our agreements. Stay within this budget and stop when the outcome is verified. Access policy and the external launch decision remain with their named owners. The agent carries planning, implementation, coordination, integration, checking, and repair within that authority.

**Storyboard**

Show a complete assignment for the partner pilot: implement and verify the journey within agreed budget; local implementation choices belong to the agent; access policy and launch stay with named owners. Animate work carrying through to evidence rather than displaying a decorative team image or requiring sign-off at every stage.

**On-screen text: Complete result / Authority and limits / Evidence**

## 03-03 Match responsibility to demonstrated capability

**Timing:** 0:46–1:13

**Narration**

Use relevant prior results to judge what the workflow can carry. Where evidence is weak, try a representative complete journey. Can it operate the interface, follow the handoff, and detect a broken result? Give it responsibility supported by that evidence, and adjust as you learn. Clear instructions help; the tools, context, and capabilities have to support them. Missing capability stays visible instead of becoming your routine testing job.

**Storyboard**

Show the agent trying a complete request journey with its actual tools. It can submit and observe a result, but initially lacks access to the receiving-team view. Make that limitation visible, then show an authorized test account providing the missing observation. Responsibility follows demonstrated capability; no routine human testing gate.

**On-screen text: Can submit / Cannot yet observe / Resolve the missing capability**

## 03-04 Resolve ordinary choices and surface consequential gaps

**Timing:** 1:13–1:33

**Narration**

A reversible layout choice is within scope: try it, check it, adjust. Missing evidence? Investigate it. A change to a shared commitment goes to the owner with the authority to settle it, with options and a recommendation. Hold only the work that depends on that choice. Being available doesn't make you the approval desk for ordinary decisions.

**Storyboard**

Show an agent improve a reversible layout and check it. Then a proposed access-policy change is placed with its named owner while the same agent continues independent display work. Include an investigation of conflicting evidence. Keep the difference between local choice and a changed shared promise visible through consequences, not a diagram of approvals.

**On-screen text: Local choice: Proceed and check / Shared change: Named owner / Other work continues**

## 03-05 Make parallel work and recovery dependable

**Timing:** 1:33–2:01

**Narration**

For larger work, agents keep assignments, ownership, dependencies, and decisions in a durable record. Choose focused parts and repositories where better understanding and independent work justify the coordination they add. Someone coordinates the combined result. After an interruption, the next worker checks what actually happened before continuing. A missing response is not a reason to repeat an action that may already be complete.

**Storyboard**

Show a compact shared work record for focused parallel contributions: Experience, Access, Operations, each with owner and current state. An interruption occurs after a transfer completes. The next worker inspects the actual completed state and resumes the next step; a second transfer is visibly avoided. Saved state is useful because it prevents duplicate work.

**On-screen text: Transfer complete / Inspect saved and actual state / Continue without repeating**

## 03-06 Return the connected result with evidence

**Timing:** 2:01–2:25

**Narration**

The workflow exercises the experience a partner will receive. It submits, accepts, refuses, and transfers a request, then checks the visible owner. If a component passes but the partner sees the wrong team, the promise is still broken. Repair and recheck. Return the result, the evidence, and the remaining limits. Release follows the authority already agreed.

**Storyboard**

Use an actual request screen to compare underlying and visible state. A component check passes and the stored owner is Operations, but the partner screen still says Access. The agent catches that mismatch, repairs the display, and repeats acceptance and refusal journeys. End with evidence that displayed and actual owners agree, plus remaining limits. No human approval gate is added.

**On-screen text: Stored owner: Operations / Displayed owner: Access / Repair and recheck**

## 03-07 Use your judgment and make it last

**Timing:** 2:25–2:47

**Narration**

You judge whether the experience serves the need and whether the checks deserve confidence. You may need a colleague's expertise or sustained time with the product. Give feedback naturally. The agent investigates, carries the correction into the work, and updates the guidance or check that should prevent a repeat. When the destination changes, reassess from today's reality.

**Storyboard**

A human owner and a colleague explore the product and challenge what the evidence actually proves. A useful observation changes both behavior and its check. Show the same correction helping a later assignment, then a changed destination producing a revised next step.

**On-screen text: Judgment / Lasting learning / Reassess the gap**

## 03-08 Make your contribution dependable for the team

**Timing:** 2:47–3:09

**Narration**

Bring peers an update they can act on: what is now true, what affects their work, and what needs judgment. Carry your commitment through, keep the agreements current, and help improve how the team works. Converge connects our shared direction with the work each of us can now lead. That is how greater individual capability can become greater collective capability.

**Storyboard**

Keep the return to peers and dependable contributions. End with Converge methodology, Build more. Together., and the repository link. No Amplifier product branding or cross-film references.

**On-screen text: Converge methodology / Build more. Together.**

# Short sample

The opening leads with human peers struggling to align increased capability, then introduces the collaboration pattern and the agent support beneath it.

## Our capacity outgrew our collaboration

AI is helping everyone on your team take on much bigger work. But working together hasn't caught up.

**Visual:** Three human peers each direct a substantial contribution with agent support. The contributions grow in quick successive reveals; the connection between them is still unsettled. Start with people, not a logo or a hypothetical perfect team.

## Progress toward different outcomes

One colleague is building for self-service. Another is preparing a staffed launch. Both are making progress toward different outcomes.

**Visual:** Two peer-owned launch cards arrive with incompatible assumptions. Highlight Self-service and Staffed launch in turn. The people are competent; their shared intent is incomplete.

## Our shared responsibility grew

Now we're making bigger calls together: what deserves investment, who owns what, and what we can count on from each other.

**Visual:** Reframe around the three human colleagues considering the shared platform. Reveal Worth doing, Clear owners, Dependable promises one at a time as the narrator names the concerns.

## Give collaboration a shared foundation

Amplifier Converge gives that collaboration a shared foundation: an outcome we agree on, complete contributions to own, and clear promises between them.

**Visual:** The peers establish a Supported pilot commitment. The experience and operations cards now refer to the same outcome, while remaining independently owned. A compact agreed promise sits between them. No central executive or approval funnel.

## Agents carry the agreements into work

Agents help expose gaps, prepare choices, and carry our agreements into coordinated, tested work.

**Visual:** Agent teammates at each human-led work area receive the same accepted commitment. Show a gap surfaced, connected work updated and the journey checked. Keep the human peers visible, with the agents doing substantive follow-through.

## Bigger work together

We spend our attention where our judgment matters most, and give each other room to deliver something bigger, together.

**Visual:** Return to the human peers discussing the connected result with their agent teammates working alongside. A brief strong closing card: Amplifier Converge, Build more. Together. Hold about one second after speech.
