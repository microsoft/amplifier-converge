# Vision

The Converge methodology helps people lead larger bodies of work together: sharing direction, owning substantial areas independently, and combining their strengths as the work changes.

As agents expand what each person can accomplish, a few peers can direct work that once needed a much larger team. The volume and speed of change can outgrow their ability to follow one another's details. Conversations that once had weeks to catch a misunderstanding may now lag behind the implementation. Collaboration has to focus on the outcomes, choices, and connections that matter across responsibilities.

A vision describes the outcome and experience we want. Contracts make essential promises explicit, including deliberate design choices. Together, they give people something they can understand, challenge, and negotiate. Focused parts with dependable connections let owners advance their areas while keeping the whole coherent. The right level of detail is whatever others need to rely on, including a precise interface or experience when it matters.

People continue to lead their areas: setting priorities and constraints, making bets, applying judgment and taste, and deciding when to change direction or stop. They draw on one another's expertise without needing everyone involved in every detail. Agents help prepare and maintain the agreements, expose missing direction, and carry authorized work through coordination, implementation, verification, and repair. The same agreements ground peer collaboration and work with agents across different tools and systems. Learning from that work can change the shared direction, promises, and boundaries.

## What we build on

**What we take as fundamental.** Human attention is limited. Producing more work does not automatically produce more value. People and agents can understand the same request differently, and individually good contributions may not work together. Plans and reports can be wrong; we need to compare what actually happens with what we intend. Both our understanding and our destination can change.

**What we choose.** Put human attention where judgment, expertise, and taste make the greatest difference. Give people and agents clear outcomes, shared promises, and real authority to carry work through. Make independent decisions the default within that authority. Organize work into understandable parts with dependable connections, and judge progress by useful results that work together. Keep enough reasoning and evidence for others to question, continue, and improve the work.

**What we assume—and need to check.** Agents can carry substantial responsibility when their capabilities, tools, context, and authority fit the work. Clear agreements and focused parts can reduce misunderstanding and coordination effort. These benefits need to show up in practice: reliable outcomes, less repeated explanation, and less work for people to rescue. Start with evidence of what participants can handle, adjust responsibility as we learn, and change practices that cost more attention than they return.

## Principles

1. **Spend attention where it changes the outcome.** Prepare consequential decisions well and protect time for deep thought.

2. **Align on intent and promises.** Expose consequential disagreements early; leave room to discover how to build.

3. **Delegate complete outcomes and real authority.** Act within agreed limits whether people are available or away. Try, check, and adjust reversible choices; prepare more carefully for decisions that are harder to undo.

4. **Make expertise available without constant involvement.** A designer can reshape a confusing experience without joining every project meeting.

5. **Derive work from the current gap.** Think navigation: recalculate from where you are toward today’s destination. Past work informs the route; it doesn’t dictate it.

6. **Make uncertainty concrete while change is cheap.** A clickable mock can reveal misunderstandings that descriptions hide.

7. **Earn confidence through evidence that can be challenged.** Examine both the result and whether the checks could reveal a broken experience.

8. **Make each intervention improve future work.** Carry decisions and reasoning forward so corrections needn’t repeat.

9. **Design systems as focused parts.** Choose boundaries that support understanding, reuse, and parallel work. Owners may direct many pieces and collaborate on the promises between them.
